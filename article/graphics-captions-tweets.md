## Tweets (a thread, in this order)

1. **11 social card**
   > Artillery started Wednesday's fire at CFB Gagetown. The base says it was reported to Range Control at 12:22 p.m.; GOES-19 saw its first heat at 12:46. It was still burning the next afternoon. The full sequence, from orbit and from the base: {link}

2. **01 region**
   > Where: the CFB Gagetown training area, N.B., about 20 km south-east of Oromocto and 9–10 km west-south-west of Gagetown village. NASA satellites picked up the fire on the afternoon of Oct. 28. {link}

3. **12 base answers**
   > CFB Gagetown told Prime Rogue: artillery shooting into the Lawfield Impact Area started it, during a Royal Canadian Artillery School course firing the 105 mm LG1. Fire danger was Moderate. About 25 ha burned. No one was hurt. {link}

4. **13 first hours**
   > Minute by minute: training restrictions at 12:15 p.m., reported to Range Control at 12:22, first heat seen from orbit at 12:46. Then five VIIRS passes from 1:22 to 3:41 p.m., peaking at 112 MW on GOES-19. {link}

5. **03 close-up**
   > 35 satellite detections in five passes, spread over about 2.6 × 1.2 km of open heath ringed by forest. The boxes are 375 m VIIRS pixels on the Oct. 27 image, the day before the fire. {link}

6. **04 SWIR**
   > In short-wave infrared, the clearing is dry, sparse ground (pink) and the forest around it is healthy (green). Every detection sits on the dry ground. {link}

7. **08 3D view**
   > The same detections in 3D, from the north: each spike is one satellite detection, its height the heat output. The fire stayed in the open clearing. {link}

8. **10 day two**
   > Day two: still smouldering overnight (1–2 MW at 4 a.m.), then burning again from midday on Oct. 29, with 13 detections, all on the southern side of the first day's area. {link}

9. **09 two-day timeline**
   > Two days of fire power over CFB Gagetown, from GOES-19 every five minutes and VIIRS on each pass. Peak 112 MW on Oct. 28 afternoon, low heat overnight, a second, smaller run on Oct. 29. {link}

10. **14 how the story broke**
    > How the CFB Gagetown fire story broke: satellites had it from 12:46 p.m., an automated watch flagged it at 5:21 p.m., and the base posted at about 9:49 p.m. The cause came the next day. {link}

11. **15 Wainwright compared**
    > Two range fires, eleven days apart. At Wainwright on Oct. 17, wind drove the fire onto soldiers; four died. At Gagetown on Oct. 28, it stayed in the impact area and no one was hurt. What changed after Wainwright? Ask "the appropriate authorities." {link}
